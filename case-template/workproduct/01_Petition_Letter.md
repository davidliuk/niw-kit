## II. Governing Standard

<!-- GUIDE: Keep this to what the reader needs; a long law section signals filler.
Re-check the current USCIS Policy Manual NIW section on uscis.gov before filing and note

### A. Burden and Standard of Proof

The petitioner must establish eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence, that is, that each claim is more likely than not true on the record as a whole. *Matter of Chawathe*, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375–76 (AAO 2010).

### B. Threshold EB-2 Classification

An advanced degree is a U.S. academic or professional degree, or a foreign equivalent, above the baccalaureate level; a U.S. baccalaureate or foreign equivalent followed by at least five years of progressive post-baccalaureate experience in the specialty is treated as equivalent to a master's degree. 8 CFR 204.5(k)(2). The petition must include an official academic record showing the degree, or the equivalent evidence. 8 CFR 204.5(k)(3)(i). The occupation must be a profession, one for which a U.S. baccalaureate or its foreign equivalent is the minimum requirement for entry. 8 CFR 204.5(k)(2); USCIS Policy Manual, Vol. 6, Pt. F, Ch. 5.

### C. National Interest Waiver

USCIS may waive the job-offer and labor-certification requirements when the petitioner shows that (1) the proposed endeavor has both substantial merit and national importance; (2) the petitioner is well positioned to advance it; and (3) on balance, it would benefit the United States to waive the requirements. *Dhanasar*, 26 I&N Dec. at 889–91; USCIS Policy Manual, Vol. 6, Pt. F, Ch. 5. The first prong examines the specific endeavor rather than the importance of the occupation or field in general. The second prong considers factors such as education, skills, knowledge, and record of success in related efforts; a model or plan for future activities; progress toward achieving the endeavor; and the interest of potential customers, users, investors, or other relevant entities. The petitioner need not show that the endeavor is more likely than not to succeed. *Dhanasar*, 26 I&N Dec. at 890.
