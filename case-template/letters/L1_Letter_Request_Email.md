## Template A: Independent User of Your Work (strongest letter type)

```text
Subject: Your use of {{YOUR_WORK_TITLE}} in {{THEIR_WORK_TITLE}} — a short factual letter?

Dear {{RECIPIENT_SALUTATION}},

I am {{APPLICANT_FULL_NAME}}, {{YOUR_ROLE_ON_WORK}} of {{YOUR_WORK_TITLE}}. I read
{{THEIR_WORK_TITLE}} ({{THEIR_WORK_ID}}) with interest, in particular
{{ONE_CHECKED_SENTENCE}}.

I am preparing a U.S. immigration petition (an EB-2 National Interest Waiver) that
documents how researchers and developers outside my own group have used this work.
Your paper is one of the clearest records of that use, but a paper cannot say how the
work came to be used or why.

Would you be willing to write a short signed letter (half a page to one page is ample)
stating only what you know first-hand: who you are, whether you have any relationship
with me (I believe we have never met or collaborated), how you came across the work,
and what you did with it? I am not asking you to endorse me, to comment on my
qualifications, or to take any position on immigration law. If a coauthor is better
placed to describe this, I would be equally grateful to hear from them.

If it saves you time, I can send a short outline of factual points drawn from your own
paper, for you to rewrite, cut, or ignore. It would help to have the letter by
{{REPLY_BY_DATE}}. If you would rather not, a one-line reply is perfectly fine and I
will not follow up.

Thank you for considering it.

Best regards,
{{APPLICANT_FULL_NAME}}
{{YOUR_PUBLIC_LINKS}}
```

## Template B: Collaborator or Supervisor (role confirmation)

```text
Subject: Request for a short factual letter about our work on {{YOUR_WORK_TITLE}}

Dear {{RECIPIENT_SALUTATION}},

I am preparing an EB-2 National Interest Waiver self-petition based on my work on
{{ENDEAVOR_SHORT_DESCRIPTION}}, including our work on {{YOUR_WORK_TITLE}}. Your
first-hand account of our collaboration would add context the public record cannot:
who did what, and what we are working on now.

If you are willing, the most useful points would be:
1. your position and research area, and how and since when we have worked together;
2. the technical problem the work addresses, in your own words;
3. what you personally observed me do on the work (design, implementation,
   experiments, writing), and what others on the team did;
4. one concrete technical decision or contribution you saw me make, and what it enabled;
5. our continuing collaboration: cadence, current joint work, and any resources (lab
   access, compute, review) available to the work, only as far as you can confirm them.

I can send a fact sheet with dates, author lists, and links so you do not have to look
anything up. Please write only what you can confirm, in your own words; a signed PDF on
letterhead, addressed "To U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services", would be ideal.
It would help to have it by {{REPLY_BY_DATE}}; if another date is more realistic,
please tell me.

Thank you very much for considering this.

Best regards,
{{APPLICANT_FULL_NAME}}
```

<!-- GUIDE: A collaborator letter proves role and continuing collaboration. It does NOT
prove independent impact; the officer treats it as interested testimony. Never let it be

discount a letter that mirrors your personal statement; ask the writer to rewrite it. -->

## Template C: Prospective or Industry User

```text
Subject: Your evaluation of {{YOUR_WORK_TITLE}} at {{ORGANIZATION}} — a short factual letter?

Dear {{RECIPIENT_SALUTATION}},

Thank you again for {{THE_INTERACTION}} (for example, the issue you opened, the
integration you described, or our call on {{DATE}}). I am preparing a U.S. immigration
petition that documents how the work is used outside my own group.

Would you be willing to write a short signed letter describing only what you know
first-hand: your role, any relationship with me, how your team came across the work,
what you evaluated or integrated, and what you plan to do with it, if anything? No
confidential details, figures, or endorsement are needed, and the letter can be in your
personal capacity if your company's policy prefers that.

I can send a short outline of the facts to save you time. If you would rather not, a
one-line reply is perfectly fine.

Best regards,
{{APPLICANT_FULL_NAME}}
```

## Follow-Up Messages

**One reminder (no reply after about a week):**

```text
Subject: Re: {{ORIGINAL_SUBJECT}}

Dear {{RECIPIENT_SALUTATION}},

A brief follow-up on my note of {{DATE}}. I understand if you are busy or would rather
not; no reply is needed in that case. If you are able to help, a short factual letter by
{{REPLY_BY_DATE}} would be very welcome.

Best regards,
{{APPLICANT_FULL_NAME}}
```

**After a "yes" (send the same day):**

```text
Subject: Re: {{ORIGINAL_SUBJECT}} — factual outline

Dear {{RECIPIENT_SALUTATION}},

Thank you very much. Attached is a one-page outline of the facts from your own paper and
the public record, for you to rewrite, shorten, or ignore. Please include only what you
know first-hand, in your own words, and correct anything that does not match your
recollection.

Practical details: a signed and dated PDF, on letterhead if your institution permits
(otherwise in your personal capacity with your title and affiliation), addressed "To
U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services". A short CV or a link to your official
profile would also help the officer understand your background.

Thank you again.

Best regards,
{{APPLICANT_FULL_NAME}}
```

**After a "no":** thank them in one line and do not follow up again.

**On receipt:** thank them, check every fact against the source, and if something is wrong, ask them to correct it; do not edit a signed letter yourself.
