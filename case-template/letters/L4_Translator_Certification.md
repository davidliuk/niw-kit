<!-- GUIDE: TRANSLATOR CERTIFICATION
Rule: any document containing foreign-language text submitted to USCIS must be
accompanied by a full English translation that the translator has certified as complete
and accurate, and by the translator's certification that they are competent to translate
from the foreign language into English. 8 CFR 103.2(b)(3). Check the current text on
eCFR before filing.

Practice:
- Translate EVERYTHING on the page: headers, seals, stamps, handwritten notes, footers,
  certificate numbers. Mark illegible text "[illegible]" and seals "[seal: text]".
- A university's own English statement on the same page is not a translator's
  certification.
- Do not certify your own translations; any competent bilingual person other than the
  applicant may sign. A professional translator is not required.
- One certification per document (or list every document it covers).
- Place in the same exhibit: cover, original, certification, translation.
- Ask whether the document is needed at all. If the EB-2 threshold rests on another
  degree, a foreign record that adds little may be better withdrawn than translated.
- The certification carries the translator's contact details and is filed. In your
  tracked repository, keep only the template; the signed copy and any translation that
  reproduces identity numbers belong in the ignored private/ or source_documents/. -->
