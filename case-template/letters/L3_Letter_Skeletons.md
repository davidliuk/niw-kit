<!-- GUIDE: LETTER SKELETONS (for the writer, not for you to sign on their behalf)
These are structures, not text to be signed unchanged. Send the relevant skeleton with
the fact sheet (L2) only if the writer asks for help; the writer rewrites it in their
own voice. Two letters with the same sentences tell the officer you wrote both.

What makes a letter count:
- Letters that mainly discuss the general importance of the field, summarize industry
  needs, or offer conclusory statements carry little weight, and a record with only a
  collaborator's letter leaves independent use unproven.
- A letter counts when it states SPECIFIC, FIRST-HAND, VERIFIABLE facts: who the writer
  is, their relationship to the applicant (including none), and what they personally
  did or saw, pointing to a public record the officer can check.
- USCIS may give opinion letters less weight when they are uncorroborated or
  inconsistent with the record; see Matter of Caron International, 19 I&N Dec. 791
  (Comm'r 1988). Pair each letter with the record it describes.

Forbidden in every letter:
- Legal conclusions ("qualifies for a National Interest Waiver", "has national
  importance", "is well positioned", "should be approved").
- Statutory boilerplate ("exceptional ability", "substantial merit", "in the national
  interest").
- Superlatives without a record ("groundbreaking", "one of the very few", "top 1%",
  "indispensable"). They are not the NIW standard and weaken the rest.
- Numbers without source, period, and method; confidential employer information.
- Claims outside the writer's knowledge (adoption by others the writer has not seen).
- Upgrading the use type: a writer who reimplemented the idea must not say they ran
  the code; a writer who compared against it must not say they adopted it.

Format: letterhead if permitted (else personal capacity, with title and affiliation),
date, addressed "To U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services", signature, printed name,
title, organization, email. Attach a short CV or link an official profile. -->

# Letter Skeletons

## Skeleton 1: Independent User (Exhibit Series I-3, or I-1 if the writer is also a senior expert)

> [Letterhead, if permitted]
>
> {{DATE}}
>
> To U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services:
>
> **Who I am.** My name is {{WRITER_NAME}}. I am {{WRITER_TITLE}} at {{WRITER_ORGANIZATION}}, where my work concerns {{WRITER_AREA}}.
>
> **Relationship.** I have no personal, academic, employment, or financial relationship with {{APPLICANT_FULL_NAME}}; we have not collaborated. *(Or state the exact limited relationship.)* I was not paid for this letter or for using the work.
>
> **How I came across the work.** I found {{YOUR_WORK_TITLE}} through {{CHANNEL}} while working on {{THEIR_PROJECT}}, and examined it because {{TECHNICAL_REASON}}.
>
> **What I did with it.** In {{THEIR_WORK_TITLE}} ({{THEIR_WORK_ID}}), my group {{EXACT_USE}}. This appears at {{PAGE_TABLE_OR_COMMIT}}.
>
> **What we found and why it mattered to our work.** {{RESULT_INCLUDING_MIXED}}. {{WHY_IT_WAS_USEFUL}}.
>
> **Since then.** *(Only if true.)* {{CONTINUED_OR_PLANNED_USE}}.
>
> I confirm that the statements above are true to the best of my knowledge.
>
> {{SIGNATURE_BLOCK}}

<!-- GUIDE: The paragraph "What I did with it" is the whole point; it should be the
longest. "Why it was useful" is where the writer's own technical judgment belongs, e.g.
(fictional) "We needed a baseline that preserved prerequisite steps; the released
library was the only public implementation we found that did so, and it ran on our
benchmark without modification." That is specific, first-hand, and checkable. -->

## Skeleton 2: Independent Expert Who Has Not Used the Work (Exhibit Series I-1)

> [Letterhead]
>
> {{DATE}}
>
> To U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services:
>
> **Who I am and my basis to assess this work.** {{WRITER_NAME}}, {{WRITER_TITLE}}, {{WRITER_ORGANIZATION}}. My research concerns {{WRITER_AREA}}; {{ONE_OR_TWO_CREDENTIALS_RELEVANT_TO_THIS_SUBFIELD}}.
>
> **Relationship.** {{NONE_OR_EXACT}}. I reviewed {{SPECIFIC_MATERIALS}} (the published paper, the public code, and the third-party papers listed below) for this letter.
>
> **The problem and current approaches.** {{TECHNICAL_EXPLANATION_IN_PLAIN_ENGLISH}}
>
> **What distinguishes the applicant's approach.** {{SPECIFIC_DIFFERENCE}}, as shown by {{RESULT_AND_PAGE}}.
>
> **Others' use that I have verified.** {{THIRD_PARTY_USE_WITH_CITATIONS}}
>
> **Assessment within my expertise.** {{SPECIFIC_TECHNICAL_ASSESSMENT}}
>
> {{SIGNATURE_BLOCK}}

<!-- GUIDE: The weakest common letter type is a senior name praising a petition they
skimmed. If you use an expert letter at all, make it explain the technical distinction
between the applicant's method and the alternatives (this directly answers "not
distinguished from ongoing research") and verify third-party use from the public
record. Prefer an expert who has a first-hand basis (ran, rebuilt, surveyed, or reviewed
the work in a public venue) over a cold reviewer. Never use paid opinion services. -->

## Skeleton 3: Collaborator or Supervisor (Exhibit Series I-2)

> [Letterhead]
>
> {{DATE}}
>
> To U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services:
>
> **Who I am and how I know the applicant.** {{WRITER_NAME}}, {{WRITER_TITLE}}, {{WRITER_ORGANIZATION}}. I have worked with {{APPLICANT_FULL_NAME}} since {{START_DATE}} as {{EXACT_ROLE}} on {{JOINT_WORKS}}.
>
> **The work.** {{TECHNICAL_PROBLEM_IN_WRITERS_WORDS}}
>
> **The applicant's role, from my own participation.** {{WHAT_THE_APPLICANT_DID}}. {{WHAT_OTHERS_INCLUDING_WRITER_DID}}.
>
> **One contribution I observed directly.** {{SPECIFIC_DECISION_AND_WHAT_IT_ENABLED}}
>
> **Continuing collaboration and resources.** {{CADENCE_CURRENT_WORK_AND_RESOURCES}}
>
> {{SIGNATURE_BLOCK}}

<!-- GUIDE: This letter proves role and an ongoing collaboration (a Prong Two factor that
is easy to leave unproven), and, if true, concrete non-monetary support such as
lab compute, shared infrastructure, or review. Ask the writer to state their own role
too; a letter that credits the applicant with everything is less credible than one with
a plausible division of labor that matches author order and the commit history. Do not
tell the writer that an officer criticized any earlier letter. -->

## Skeleton 4: Employer Factual Letter (HR verification in C-1; manager letter in C-1 or C-2)

> [Company letterhead]
>
> {{DATE}}
>
> **Re: Verification of Employment, {{APPLICANT_FULL_NAME}}**
>
> This letter confirms that {{APPLICANT_FULL_NAME}} is employed by {{EMPLOYER}} as {{TITLE}}, full time, since {{START_DATE}}, and is currently employed.
>
> *(Manager version only.)* I am {{MANAGER_TITLE}} and have supervised {{APPLICANT_SHORT_NAME}} since {{DATE}}. {{APPLICANT_SHORT_NAME}}'s individual responsibilities include {{TWO_OR_THREE_NONCONFIDENTIAL_RESPONSIBILITIES}}. This letter omits confidential technical and business information.
>
> {{SIGNATURE_BLOCK}}

<!-- GUIDE: Ask HR for the standard verification letter; it is easy to get and proves
dates, title, and full-time status. Ask for a version without salary for the filing.
A manager letter is optional: category-level duties only, no internal system names or
unpublished metrics, and checked against company policy on statements to government
agencies. Any figure needs source, period, method, and the applicant's share, or it goes.
An employer letter cannot carry national importance; the officer looks for benefit
beyond one employer. -->
